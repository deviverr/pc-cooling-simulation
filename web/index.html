    Your parts and assumptions

    PartProfileDimensions / assumptions

    Before you buy parts

    Use your exact case, motherboard, GPU, cooler and PSU manuals to verify the plan. Manufacturer TDP and board-power figures are reference values; partner-card dimensions and connectors can differ. Imported and custom profiles use your own assumptions.

    The airflow model is a coarse steady pressure projection. Heat colors are qualitative; temperature cards use a separate thermal estimate. This tool helps compare layouts and catch planning issues, and does not replace physical measurements.

    THE MODEL

    A planning studio for your PC.

    Start with a preset or choose component profiles under Build. The physical side panel and its display are separate: cutaway mode reveals the interior while a closed panel still blocks simulated flow. Use Flow maps for section views, Power & wiring for PSU planning, and Build report for the inputs and fit flags.

    This is an interactive, qualitative sandbox, not validated CFD or a hardware temperature predictor. Procedural 3D parts approximate your photo; exact case, components, dimensions and fan directions were not identified.

    Fan free-air ratings scale with RPM, fan size and a pressure-oriented profile. A case/filter/radiator restriction factor reduces the nominal flow. Through-case exchange uses a simplified intake/exhaust network with passive leakage. Pressure bias compares fan capacities; it is not pressure in pascals. Moved fans are coupled to their assigned wall mount by distance and orientation; internal or sideways fans contribute less to estimated case exchange. Rotating a fan to face the opposite way reverses its estimated inlet/outlet role. This is an illustrative assumption. Free placement permits overlaps and does not guarantee hardware fit.

    Air temperature rise follows ΔT = Q / (ρ cₚ V̇), using ρ = 1.2 kg/m³ and cₚ = 1005 J/(kg·K). CPU/GPU estimates add assumed cooler thermal resistance. Liquid cooling models pump-dependent block resistance and radiator rejection. Numerical temperatures are illustrative, uncalibrated estimates.

    The airflow view now solves a coarse 3D pressure field across the PC and surrounding room air. Fan actuators move existing air. A finite-volume pressure projection reduces divergence, solid panels and the GPU PCB block flow, and heatsinks/radiators act as permeable regions. Both the CPU cooler and GPU fans actively move air. Closed panels still have modeled vents and small leakage openings.

    Visible paths are traced upstream into the room, then followed through inlets, component heatsinks and exhausts. Every displayed path starts outside the case. Tracer speed follows local field strength; color indicates qualitative heat pickup or relative speed. CPU and GPU fan speeds are editable under Conditions. With no fan power, the model retains a weak assumed buoyancy term.

    This is a steady, coarse, inviscid-style pressure projection with empirical fan forcing and porous resistance. It does not solve the full transient Navier–Stokes equations, turbulence, blade aerodynamics or conjugate heat transfer. Temperature cards still use the separate simplified thermal model. Detailed physical predictions require measured fan curves and validated geometry.

    Pressure projection is calculated when the build changes.

    Editor controls

    Select a fan in the object list or 3D view. G shows move handles, R shows rotation rings, and X/Y/Z restricts the visible axis. Drag a colored handle, or enter millimeters and degrees in the inspector. Snap uses 10 mm and 15° steps. Use Ctrl/Cmd+Z to undo, Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z to redo, Ctrl/Cmd+D to duplicate, Delete to remove, F to focus, and Esc to deselect. Transformations persist in exported builds.
